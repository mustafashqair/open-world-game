# His Three.js game versus five browser 3D peers

6 November 2026. Target: Samsung Galaxy A14, 4 GB, Mali-G57; goal 30 fps (33.3 ms/frame).

**The earlier peer teardown did not make this comparison. This document does.** His build already implements many of its generic recommendations. The remaining opportunities are to complete and budget those systems, rather than add workers, instancing, pooling or a fixed timestep from scratch. Priorities below are engineering hypotheses for this particular build, not measured A14 speedups.

### Supplied measurements, used once rather than retested

The earlier very-low run transferred **~71.86 MB before input unlock**, with no additional measured transfer during play. WebGL programs increased **229→267**; **38 linkProgram calls** occurred after playable despite ~91 seconds warm-up. Scene objects **4,977→6,923**, skinned meshes **194→249**, instanced meshes **69→75**, terrain chunks **18→42**. Median **107 draw calls / 305,773 triangles per frame**. Frame intervals **p50 283ms, p95 383ms, worst1,217ms**. These are the task's supplied observations, on software rendering and CPU throttling; no new live measurements were made.

These facts motivate investigation of first-use hitches and residency. They do **not** attribute a CPU/GPU bottleneck, establish a leak, or establish the same regression in pinned v50g2. In particular v50g2 adds city fog/culling, villager throttling and additional Lambert-template compilation that may address part of the earlier evidence.

## Ranked five changes for this game

Ranked by expected relevance to his evidence, including startup, hitching and 4GB memory pressure. This is not an experimentally established ranking of steady-state FPS gains. First reproduce each applicable symptom on the exact release being changed; use existing counters rather than rerunning the supplied baseline as though it were new evidence.

### 1. Close shader variant coverage and coordinate first-use uploads

**Current:** older live build added 38 programs after a 91 s warm-up. Pinned code already has compileAsync, light-pool stabilization, warm vertex batches 250k/350k/600k, 12ms texture-init slices and g2pre synchronous compilation (H2:3439–3500). Terrain worker completion can immediately attach geometry (H1:399). This calls for completing an existing pipeline, not adding a second blanket warm-up.

**Peer:** Rally/Madalin store 2 ms async upload slices with 4 MiB buffers [R/M]. Those engine settings are not measured 2 ms GPU work and cannot simply be copied into Three.js.

**Concrete change:** record a material/program variant inventory keyed by actual light/shadow counts, fog, skinning, instancing, transparency and material conversion. Warm the variants used by the selected release/preset and verify which programs first appear after input. Queue worker-result attachment, texture initialization and asset visibility behind one scheduler with a provisional **~2 ms CPU submission slice plus a byte cap**, limiting individual resource sizes and decode concurrency. Precompile newly converted Lambert/LOD variants before revealing them, using compileAsync where supported. Keep a last-ready proxy visible until the replacement is ready. Retain the existing pre-play warm-up; don't merely lengthen it or repeat the entire world visit.

**Risk:** JS cannot preempt one large GPU upload or synchronous compiler call; excessive prewarming increases startup and memory. Deferred objects can pop in. v50g2 may already have fixed some older misses, so additional warm work must be justified by its variant trace.

**HIGH policy:** safe for HIGH when it preserves the exact materials, lighting and ready assets, with no visible coverage gaps. A policy that drops effects or substitutes visibly cheaper materials must be LOW/MEDIUM-gated. Validate startup duration as well as post-play program creation and hitch percentiles.

### 2. Ship GPU-compressed textures, rather than resizing WebP only after decoding

**Current:** measured zero compressed textures; KTX2Loader already installed. TCAP decodes then downsizes ordinary textures to256 on very-low/512 mobile, and low/vlow character textures to512 (H0:25–47). That reduces eventual GPU allocation but still entails source download/decode and resize work. Standalone repo GLBs predominantly embed WebP.

**Peer:** Rally/Madalin package GPU-compressed DXT/BC textures [R/M]. Madalin stores mip streaming; Rally does not. Neither proves an Android-optimal codec or universal512 MiB target. **KTX2/Basis is our Three.js implementation choice, not a recovered common peer format.**

**Concrete change:** convert the largest resident color/normal/ORM textures to mipmapped KTX2/Basis with correct color spaces; wire the existing GLTF KTX2 path to assets that actually use it. Supply pre-sized LOW/MEDIUM variants to avoid full-source decode/downscale peaks. Select transcode targets from real device support; keep unsupported-format fallback. Audit texture bytes and references across scene, templates and caches; retire duplicate decoded images when safely unused. Keep meshopt: the repository already uses it, so adding Draco is not the main gap.

**Risk:** transcode startup cost, alpha/normal artifacts, colorspace errors, temporary decode peaks and codec-specific quality. Unsupported texture targets must not fail loading. WebP transfer bytes may already be small; expected benefit is also GPU/decoded memory, not just the 71.86 MB network figure.

**HIGH policy:** compression can be enabled on HIGH after side-by-side visual validation at its original resolution and suitable encoding quality. Lower texture resolution and aggressive lossy settings remain LOW/MEDIUM-gated. Measure peak loading memory, upload hitches and resident bytes separately.

### 3. Extend terrain's spatial bounds into a world-wide residency budget

**Current:** 128-unit chunks and radius+1 eviction/disposal already exist. Very-low view3 has an81-position retained-square upper bound, not an81-chunk steady-state promise. Earlier objects/skinned meshes/chunks grew during traversal. G50 deliberately **keeps the distant city loaded but hidden**, stopping its village logic, to avoid reconstruction hitches (H1:4431–4437; H2:3553–3557). Templates/preloads also retain resources. A global byte ceiling across these systems was not recovered.

**Peer:** Slow Roads declares finite heightmap entry counts12–40 and impostor/tree capacities400–16000 /400–525 [SR]. Entry count is not a memory figure, and its scene scale differs.

**Concrete change:** add ownership-aware counters for live chunk geometry, merged copies, textures, model templates and retained settlement assets. Introduce active/prefetch/retained tiers with an LRU byte budget, retaining reusable templates and nearby proxies while releasing cold unique resources. Preserve shared materials/geometries until reference counts reach zero. Give queued terrain jobs a generation/preset token and reject obsolete completions before attaching them. Pool reusable buffers/instance storage where a traversal trace shows churn. Preserve the city-retention optimization within a measured budget; don't blindly revert to rebuilding it on every return.

**Risk:** overaggressive eviction recreates the hitches the current keep-city system was designed to avoid. Incorrect shared-resource disposal breaks still-visible assets. Too-small rings cause pop-in; byte estimates omit driver overhead.

**HIGH policy:** safe for HIGH if visible/prefetched content and fast-return behavior fit the budget. Fewer visible objects, shorter render radius or early eviction within visibility must be LOW/MEDIUM-gated. Validate a long out-and-back route for a plateau in memory and acceptable return hitches; raw growth from18 to42 chunks alone is not a failure criterion.

### 4. Make the 30 fps target and automatic pixel policy use real presentation time

**Current:** vlow ratio .55 is already conservative. MEDIUM 1.5/HIGH 2 remain viewport-dependent. A 25 ms rAF gate is labelled 30 fps; auto-quality uses clamped gameplay time even after the displayed FPS was fixed to raw time (H2:3607–3627). There is no recovered backing-pixel-area ceiling.

**Peer:** Swooop fixes1280×720; Slow Roads uses scale1/native DPR off and offers an explicit 30 fps option [S/SR]. PolyTrack defaults to native DPR, so it is evidence for an independent control, not a low-resolution mobile default.

**Concrete change:** pace an explicit 30 fps mode from elapsed presentation timestamps with a **1000/30 ms deadline**, retaining phase/remainder instead of assuming60Hz rAF. Keep simulation timestep/interpolation independent. Drive adaptation from raw frame intervals and a rolling window with hysteresis, not clamped `dt`. Add an independent maximum backing-pixel budget to `min(S.pr,DPR,sqrt(pixelBudget/(cssWidth*cssHeight)))`; reapply on resize/orientation. Trial ~0.9 M pixels only as a Swooop-inspired starting experiment, not a validated A14 threshold. Log effective width×height, ratio, cap, preset and quality mode. Preserve his current .55 floor until device experiments justify changing it; don't reduce resolution merely because the software-rendering baseline was slow.

**Risk:** blur, resizing reallocations, adaptive oscillation, and user confusion if saved HIGH silently changes appearance. A pixel fix will not resolve CPU-bound skinning/AI work. At very-low.55 the area cap may do nothing, which is expected.

**HIGH policy:** accurate timing/accounting is safe for HIGH. Reduced pixel density or automatic downgrade visibly changes HIGH and must be opt-in or LOW/MEDIUM-gated; leave HIGH's original content/resolution available. Check 60/90/120 Hz displays and input response separately.

### 5. Unify projected-size LOD and active-animation budgets across actor families

**Current:** geometry NPC thresholds 10/22/34 with 3-unit hysteresis, village skeleton cap 5/8/14, nearest-six and distance mixer throttles already exist (H0:1707; H1:4435–4441; H2:3548–3552,3574–3583). Earlier 194→249 skinned meshes are resident counts, **not evidence all249 mixers or skeletons updated every frame**. v50g2 already hides/stops distant noncombat villagers; don't propose that as new work.

**Peer:** Rally LOD biases .4/1/2 and Madalin thresholds .849371/.295664 express screen-relative LOD; Slow Roads separately budgets detail/density/capacities [R/M/SR]. Peers did not establish a matching open-world character-animation budget.

**Concrete change:** retain those existing optimizations, then make visual LOD respond to projected bounds size and selected backing-buffer height, with hysteresis and object-class thresholds. Add one measured active-animation/skeleton budget shared by villagers, guards, bandits, horses and fauna, using existing throttles as inputs. Prioritize player, combat opponents, dialogue and mount; keep authoritative collision/AI state independent of visual updates. Distant noninteractive actors can use existing far geometry/impostors; stagger visual updates and preserve accumulated animation time. Only add this cross-family scheduler if profiles show these updates remain material after current v50g2 culling.

**Risk:** silhouette changes, animation stepping, silhouette/collision mismatch, incorrect combat prioritization and state desynchronization. A centralized scheduler can add bookkeeping overhead. Projected-size thresholds require calibration; Unity numbers cannot be pasted into Three.js distances.

**HIGH policy:** measurement, shared accounting and invisible-actor scheduling can be safe for HIGH. More aggressive geometry swaps, fewer visible actors and reduced visible animation rates must be LOW/MEDIUM-gated unless HIGH visuals are explicitly accepted. Validate dense combat and mounted travel, not only an empty landscape.

## Where his build is already at or better than the recovered peer patterns

- **Low-cost pixels and shadows:** very-low .55/low .75 with no dynamic shadows are already more conservative than the recovered PolyTrack defaults and Slow Roads' default scale 1. This does not imply better FPS in a more complex world.
- **Bounded simulation plus interpolation:** 60 Hz player simulation and a four-step accumulator cap are already implemented; the cap is tighter than the peer catch-up settings. Recommending fixed-step physics as missing would be incorrect.
- **Actual terrain workers:** transferable geometry and 1–3 workers already exist. P only established a dedicated simulation worker for one of five peers and no OffscreenCanvas rendering for any. He does not need a worker renderer merely to match them.
- **Mesh compression, instancing and batching:** meshopt is present in 196 of 218 inspected standalone GLBs, alongside existing InstancedMesh, far-tree representations, terrain batching and time-budgeted MERGE48. Another generic “use Draco/instancing” recommendation misses the implementation already in place.
- **Explicit pools and low-tier animation policy:** blood rings, finite ground pools, fixed light counts, village skeleton limits and mixer throttles are more explicit than most recovered peer evidence. Pooling and distance LOD are already features, not discoveries left to implement.
- **Startup preparation and adaptability:** compileAsync, real warm draws, texture preparation and automatic preset downgrade already exist; none of the peers established an automatic dynamic-resolution controller. The relevant gap is correctness/coverage/budgeting, not the absence of preparation.

Dynamic shadows are deliberately outside the top five: the measured tier has them disabled. Shorter MEDIUM shadow coverage or a 512 map is a valid later experiment; removing HIGH cascades would be a visible artistic tradeoff, and Madalin's 15-unit setting is not a universal mobile target.

## Limits and validation boundary

1. Stored Unity settings can be overridden at runtime. Current quality index does not prove a mobile startup choice; mip budgets do not prove actual texture residency; DXT/BC inventories do not prove Android support. Swooop's inactive 1024 maps are not rendering costs. Slow Roads scene tables must not be combined into a fictitious universal configuration.
2. Loader support does not prove asset usage. The supplied zero-compressed-texture observation is stronger than bundled KTX2Loader.
